## Phase 3: the core build (Milestones 4-7, completed 2026-11-06)

**Where we are.** The working system exists on a simulated hotel group's data: a fake loyalty platform to talk to, the old way of
working (arm A), a safe way to change points that every workflow shares, and the improved way of working without AI (arm B).

**What you can see.** An agent opens a case and presses Investigate. The system reads the member, the stay, the points ledger and
the queue of points still on their way, and says what the written policy allows, with a link to the exact paragraph. To credit points,
a second person must approve the exact amount, and the system checks the facts again before anything moves.

**What we don't know yet.** Whether arm B is better than arm A, or by how much. We wrote our predictions down before building it, and
we haven't scored it, on purpose. Nothing here is a measured saving.

**Risk you should know about.** The old way is deliberately kept as it was, flaws included, and a test fails if it changes. That makes
the later comparison fair, and it means arm A stays visibly worse in the demo. Arm B also asks members for more proof of identity
than arm A does, so some simple requests may take longer.

**Decision needed from you.** None now. The next phase adds AI, and its first live run costs a small amount of API money, so it starts
with a request for your go-ahead and a spending limit.

**Next.** The AI layer (Milestone 8), security hardening (Milestone 9), then the benchmark that compares all three (Milestone 10).

## Phase 2: the data and the day-one picture (Milestone 3)

**Outcome.** We built a realistic, fully synthetic copy of the loyalty queue and its systems, and read 12 months of simulated history the way we'd read a client's real export on day one.

**Evidence (synthetic, from documented assumptions).**
- 3,872 requests in a year; 27% ended with the wrong points.
- Estimated cost: about $13,078 over-credited and 6,521,570 points members were owed but didn't get (about $52,173).
- Our prediction: about 61% of the failures can be fixed without AI, and about 21% need AI; the rest are split between the two or can't be fixed.

**Risks.** These numbers restate our own assumptions; a real client's export would replace them. The person writing the test data also builds the solutions, so 30 labels go to an independent review (the owner).

**Decisions needed now.** None. Next: review 30 labels (about 45 minutes).

**Cost so far.** $0 in services. No AI usage.

## Phase 1: discovery and experiment design (Milestones 1–2, completed 2026-10-28)

**Outcome.** We know what to measure and how we'll decide. We wrote down, before building anything, which problems
the current loyalty support tools have, which fix should solve each one (better tools or AI), and what results would
count as a win.

**Evidence.**
- 25 likely problems, each with a predicted fix; our own prediction is that **most are fixed without AI**, and AI
  could help with about 1 in 8 requests (the messy ones: several stays in one message, other languages, receipts).
- Seven success tests, including a hard rule: no points may ever move twice or without a supervisor.
- These were locked in a dated record before any software existed, so results can't be bent to fit.

**Risks.**
- Everything so far is a simulation; the volumes and costs are assumptions.
- The person designing the test also builds the solutions. We reduce that risk by locking the design first and
  including cases where AI shouldn't help.

**Decisions needed now.** None. Later: approving a small budget for live AI tests (Milestone 8) and whether to host a
public demo (Milestone 12).

**Cost so far.** $0 in services. No AI usage yet.
